₹149. That's what we charge someone to find out, before they ever talk to us, whether their business is losing customers to a bad website or a system that tracks nothing.

The first version was a plain form: three free-text boxes, submit, done. Clean on paper, but nobody treated it like a real test, just another contact form with a fee attached.

Between October 29 and 31 we tore it apart and rebuilt it as nine screens. One question each, a visible progress bar, mostly single-tap choices: how old the business is, what a customer finds when they Google it right now, how fast someone actually gets a reply to a message. One question asks you to rate your own confidence, 1 to 5, that you aren't losing business from something online. Most people have never had to answer that out loud.

Name, phone, email, the identity questions, moved to the very last screen. Anyone still there at question 9 isn't a tire-kicker.

Charging ₹149 before any sales conversation sounds backwards for a services business that needs leads. It isn't. Someone willing to pay and finish nine honest questions about their own business is a sharper lead than a free "request a quote" form produces, and we get an actual report to write instead of a one-line enquiry.

We'll know in a few weeks if the bet holds.
