# RabbitLore Research Findings — 2026-11-02

## Executive Summary & Opportunity

**Top finding this week: Meta is testing "Threads Friends" ("Thriends") — a Close-Friends-style,
capped-at-50, invisible-to-the-rest-of-the-platform posting mode — inside Threads itself, live in
Ireland, Italy and Japan as of Oct 30 2026.[1][2]** This is the first time in this research line
that the Pillar 1 "private beats public for Gen Z" thesis has been externally validated by the
single largest social-media owner in the world, not by another indie or funded challenger. Meta
itself is now shipping a small-group, non-algorithmic, invisible-to-outsiders posting surface —
the exact shape of product RabbitLore, Yope, Retro and Posting2 have all separately bet on. The
feature mirrors Instagram's 2018 Close Friends and Twitter's discontinued 2022 Circles (capped at
150, killed ~1 year later), so the caution from the 2026-10-22 MIDiA nuance — don't overclaim
"private wins everywhere" — still applies: Meta bolting a privacy mode onto an otherwise public,
ad-supported, algorithmic platform is not Meta becoming a private-first app. But as a pure demand
signal it is the strongest external validation to date: the platform with the most data on what
Gen Z actually wants concluded the open/broadcast default alone isn't enough.

**Second finding, the closest structural peer RabbitLore has found to date: A/SIDE, a new, tiny,
fully unfunded ("no investors," per its own site) private-social app from a 4-person Minnesota
product studio, Lab 1908.[3][4][5][6]** A/SIDE matches RabbitLore's own thesis on more axes than
Yope, Retro, or Posting2 do: invite-only mutual-connection feed (no public profiles, no discovery,
no search for users), strict reverse-chronological order, no ads, no AI training on content, and
— a step further than any prior watchlist entry — open-source app and server code. It charges
$20/year for full history beyond a free 30-day window, no VC. It is not a competitive threat at
its current size (one studio, no funding, no named download numbers found), but it is the best
available proxy for testing specific product bets cheaply by watching what a structurally similar,
resource-constrained team chooses to build next.

**Third finding, continuity, not new: no on-topic Reddit discussion of RabbitLore, Yope, Highrise,
or "private social Gen Z" queries surfaced this run** (`REDDIT_SEARCH_ACROSS_SUBREDDITS` against
r/GenZ-adjacent and competitor-named subreddits returned only off-topic noise — scam-checker bots,
unrelated AI-companion/relationship threads, game dev posts) — same null pattern as every recent
run. `COMPOSIO_SEARCH_TRENDS`, `COMPOSIO_SEARCH_NEWS`, and `COMPOSIO_SEARCH_WEB` actions were
attempted first per the standing escalation from 2026-10-26/29, and succeeded this run (no
`ToolkitDisabled` restriction this time) for the trends query but general web_search was used for

**Top Opportunity:** Meta's Threads Friends test is the strongest demand-side validation Pillar 1
has received to date — bigger signal than one more funded/unfunded indie app, because it shows the
market's biggest incumbent concluding the public-feed-only model is losing specific behavior, not
just that smaller teams can find a private-social niche. The concrete move: RabbitLore's next
content piece (LinkedIn, once the 2+ run publishing backlog clears — see performance loop below)
should lead with Meta's move, not another indie-app funding figure, framed as "the biggest platform
on earth just agreed with the bet we made before any of these existed" — and should explicitly
differentiate on what Meta structurally cannot offer inside Threads: no ads anywhere in the product,
no algorithm anywhere (not just in the friends-mode), no public graph underneath, and night-gating
as a behavioral ritual, not a toggle. A/SIDE goes on the watchlist as a new entry, tracked for
product-level signal (cheap to watch, structurally closest peer), not treated as a threat.

## 2. Trending in the Niche

### Meta tests "Threads Friends" / "Thriends" — platform-level validation of Pillar 1

Threads (Meta) is piloting a feature that lets a user share posts with up to 50 selected
followers ("Thriends"), invisible to everyone else on the platform, currently live only in
Ireland, Italy and Japan.[1][2] Mechanics: users build a curated friends list (Meta auto-suggests
frequently-interacted accounts, user can edit), tapping a green bubble surfaces friends-only posts
from the last 24 hours. The feature explicitly echoes Instagram's 2018 Close Friends and Twitter's
2022 Circles (capped at 150, discontinued ~1 year later) — a structural reminder that bolt-on
privacy features on top of public/algorithmic/ad-funded platforms have a mixed track record
(Circles failed partly because "your close friends also have to be on the platform and posting to
their own lists," a coordination problem [per Dataconomy's analysis][2]). Meta has not said when
or whether the test expands beyond the 3 pilot countries.

Why this matters for Pillar 1: this is the first *platform-incumbent* validation of RabbitLore's
core bet, as distinct from indie/funded-challenger validation (Yope, Retro, Posting2, all already
tracked). It raises the ceiling on how seriously the "private beats public" thesis should be taken
in any external-facing copy — but it does not collapse RabbitLore's differentiation, because Meta's
version keeps the ads, the algorithm (everywhere except the one friends-mode surface), and the
public graph underneath. The caution from 2026-10-22 (MIDiA: market bifurcating, not converging
one-way on private) is reinforced here in a new way: even Meta is treating "private" as an add-on
mode inside a public product, not a replacement for it.

### A/SIDE — new, closest-yet structural peer, unfunded

A/SIDE (Lab 1908, a 4-person independent product studio based in Saint Paul, Minnesota, "no
investors," per the company's own site) launched/updated to version 1.6 in late October 2026.[3][6]
Core mechanics, per its own site and App Store listing: invite-only mutual connections (no public
profiles, no user directory, no search), strict reverse-chronological feed, end-to-end encrypted
direct messages (legacy conversations may be unencrypted), no ads, no AI training on user content,
independently-hosted servers/storage outside AWS/Google Cloud/Azure, and — the one axis none of
Yope/Retro/Posting2 match — fully open-source app and server code (GitHub: A-SIDE-Social/aside).[4]
Monetization: free tier with 30-day history, Pro $20/year for full archive, Pro Family $60/year
for up to 6 people, no monthly plans.[3][6] No funding, no investor backing, no named download/user
count found in any source checked this run (RuntimeWire's coverage, syndicated from Hacker News,
frames it explicitly as "testing whether privacy terms and a low annual subscription can support
social software without ads, algorithmic ranking or venture-scale growth targets"[4] — i.e. A/SIDE
itself is testing a thesis adjacent to, not identical to, RabbitLore's).

Why this matters: A/SIDE is the first entry on this watchlist that matches RabbitLore's thesis on
privacy/no-algorithm/no-ads *and* adds a dimension (open-source, zero public discovery surface)
RabbitLore doesn't currently claim. It's too small and unfunded to be a competitive threat, but it
is a free, cheap-to-watch proxy for product decisions — if a team with the same constraints and
instincts ships a feature, that's a signal worth noting before RabbitLore commits engineering time
to the same idea, independent of whether A/SIDE itself ever gets traction.

## Competitive Edge

**What are they doing that we aren't?** Meta is validating the exact demand RabbitLore already
bet on, at platform scale, inside the world's largest social app family. A/SIDE is doing something
genuinely adjacent to RabbitLore on more axes than any prior watchlist entry: full open-source code
and zero public discovery surface (no profiles, no search, no directory at all) — RabbitLore's own
docs describe peer-to-peer lore-matching and night-gating as the differentiating mechanic, not
open-sourcing the codebase or eliminating all forms of discovery; worth naming as a real gap, even
if small.

**Is that gap worth closing?** The Meta finding is not a gap to close — it's validation to cite,
not a feature to copy (RabbitLore cannot and should not try to out-Meta Meta on reach). The A/SIDE
open-source gap is NOT worth closing for RabbitLore right now: open-sourcing the app is a
trust/transparency play suited to a privacy-first brand positioning itself against Big Tech
surveillance specifically (A/SIDE's own pitch: "you can look under the hood") — RabbitLore's
positioning doc centers peer-matching and night-gating as the emotional/behavioral hook, not
infrastructure transparency, so chasing this would be copying a different brand's trust mechanic
without the audience actually asking for it. No source checked this run showed Gen Z users citing
"open source" as a reason they chose or stayed on a private-social app.

**What's the concrete move?** (1) Next LinkedIn/Reddit piece leads with Meta's Threads Friends test
as the headline proof point for Pillar 1, explicitly naming what Meta's version structurally can't
match (ads-everywhere-else, algorithm-everywhere-else, public graph underneath) rather than treating
it as a threat. (2) Add A/SIDE to the watchlist as a tracked, not-chased peer — useful for cheap
product-signal-watching given its structural similarity, explicitly noted as non-threat given zero
funding/traction. (3) Do not add an open-source commitment to the roadmap based on this week's
evidence alone — flagged as "we will NOT do this now," revisit only if a future research run finds
actual Gen Z demand for app-code transparency specifically, not inferred from A/SIDE's own
marketing copy.
