# RabbitLore Research Findings — 2026-10-10

## Executive Summary & Opportunity

**Top finding this week: the "private social app" / "close friends app" search-interest signal
that's been climbing all year just dropped sharply in the most recent two weeks.** Google Trends
(COMPOSIO_SEARCH_TRENDS, 12-month window): "private social app" ran 56→28→**7**→**5** (partial)
across the last 4 weeks (Jul 5–11 through Oct 4–10, 2026) — down from a 100-peak in early June.
"close friends app" shows the same shape: 70→38→**18**→**16** (partial) over the same 4 weeks.
Both queries had spent the prior ~5 months climbing (mid-40s to a 100-peak in May/June), so this
is a real reversal, not noise within a flat baseline — read as cooling interest in the broad
"private social" search category after its summer peak, not a RabbitLore-specific signal, but
directly relevant to how much weight Pillar 1 (night-gated private lore sharing) should put on
"the macro trend is accelerating" framing going forward — it should not claim acceleration right
now. No RabbitLore-side search/traffic data exists to cross-check against (see §1).

**Also new this week: a documented real-world wave of regulatory-driven migration to exactly this
app category.** News search surfaced extensive, dated coverage (9 separate outlets, Nov 2025–Jun
2026) of teens in Australia and the UK moving to private/algorithm-light apps — **Yope, Lemon8,
and a newly-surfaced name, Coverstar** — as Australia's under-16 social media ban took effect
(Dec 2025) and the UK announced its own ban for under-16s (announced 2026-06-15)[8]. Hard numbers:
Australia's ban caused **4.7 million accounts removed** across 10 platforms as of the first
government report[9]; Yope and Lemon8 "shot up the app store charts" in the week before the
Australian ban took effect[2]; by the ban's start date, Yope, Lemon8, and Coverstar were named
together as the three apps teens were piling into[3][4][5]. This substantially strengthens the
existing regulatory-pressure read (first flagged 2026-09-28, corroborated 2026-10-01) with a real,
measurable before/after number (4.7M accounts) rather than just a direction.

**Top Opportunity:** the regulatory/migration wave is not new direction for RabbitLore (the
existing Pillar 1 framing already anticipated it), but the Coverstar discovery and the 4.7M-account
hard number are genuinely new evidence worth a watchlist entry and a Competitive Edge read — see
below. The trends reversal is the more consequential item for strategy.md: it means Pillar 1
should stop citing "rising category interest" as supporting evidence until a future run shows the
dip was temporary, since the two most recent snapshot points (not one) now show decline.

## 3. Competitive Edge

1. **What are they doing that we aren't?** Two things surfaced this week. (a) The regulatory wave
   now has a hard number behind it — 4.7M accounts removed in Australia alone[9] — that none of
   RabbitLore's 5 watchlisted competitors can point to as validation, because none of them are
   actually benefiting from age-gating the way Yope/Lemon8/Coverstar are (they're explicitly named
   as the destinations). (b) **Coverstar** is a genuinely new name, built specifically for the
   age band being regulated out of mainstream platforms, with safety-first design (no DMs at
   all, AI+human moderation)[6][7] as its core differentiator — a stricter trust/safety posture
   than even RabbitLore's night-gating.
2. **Is this gap worth closing for us?** The regulatory-wave validation is reassurance, same read
   as Yope's funding story in `research/2026-10-01.md` — it validates "private/safe beats public"
   as a working macro-thesis, not a reason to change what RabbitLore builds. Coverstar itself is
   **not worth chasing**: its audience is Gen Alpha (9–16), younger than RabbitLore's stated
   audience (overthinkers, writers, nerds, "lonely kids who grew up" — adults reflecting on
   childhood, not children themselves, per `docs/positioning.md`), its core loop is public
   short-video content with avatar customization as a side feature, and its "no DMs at all"
   safety model solves a child-safety problem RabbitLore's night-gated peer-matching model
   doesn't share in the first place. Copying its no-DM/AI-moderation design would import a child-
   safety-specific constraint built for a different regulatory target, not a feature RabbitLore's
   actual (adult/young-adult overthinker) audience is asking for.
3. **What's the concrete move?** Add Coverstar to `competitors/watchlist.md` under the existing
   "private-social wave" category (next to Yope, Locket Widget, noplace, Ville from
   `research/2026-10-01.md`) as a tracked-but-not-competed-with entry — same treatment, not a new
   pillar, no copy change this week. Separately, flag the search-interest reversal (§2 above) to
   strategy.md so Pillar 1's evidence language stops implying the category is still accelerating —
   this is a correction to existing language, not a new recommendation, and it should happen now
   rather than let a stale "rising" claim sit in the living strategy doc for another cycle.
