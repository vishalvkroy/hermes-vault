# RabbitLore Research Findings — 2026-10-22

## Executive Summary & Opportunity

**Top finding this week: a new, well-funded "anti-feed" competitor (Retro) shows the "private
beats public" macro-thesis is now commercially proven by real revenue, not just funding
announcements.** Retro — a friends-only photo-journal app built by ex-Instagram engineers
(Nathan Sharp, Ryan Olson) — closed a $21M Series A (led by Field Ventures and Thrive Capital) in
Aug 2026[1], and by mid-October had reached 7M downloads on that same raise[2]. Retro runs a
subscription model and Engadget's own hands-on coverage describes it as working "much like
Instagram used to," sharing photos only with a select few rather than a public feed[3]. This is
the first run with a second, independently-dated confirmation (TechCrunch Aug 28[1], then a
follow-up legal/VC newsletter piece in October[2]) of the same funded competitor — the same
"repeat the finding before treating it as a pattern" bar this research line already applies to
its own trend reads.

**Second finding, a balancing nuance from an industry-analyst source, not a reversal: MIDiA
Research (Oct 21, 2026) reports that in their own Q2 2026 consumer survey, "entertainment" has
overtaken "connecting with friends" as the most-cited reason Western users give for using social
media, with platforms bifurcating into entertainment-first (TikTok/YouTube) versus peer-to-peer
apps[4].** This doesn't contradict Pillar 1 — RabbitLore sits squarely in the peer-to-peer half
of that split, which MIDiA still treats as a real, surviving category — but it's a useful caution
against over-claiming "private is simply winning everywhere": the market is splitting in two
directions, not converging on one[4].

**Third finding, category context only, not a watchlist addition: a new UK "superapp" (ZYMIX)
launched nationwide Sep 28 2026 targeting university students, bundling messaging, a social
feed, payments, and events into one app[5] — the opposite direction (consolidation) from
RabbitLore's and the rest of the watchlist's atomization/privacy direction.** A Guardian
investigation (Oct 13 2026) separately reports ZYMIX runs on servers owned by a Chinese firm on a
US blacklist, a real security/trust liability for that product specifically[6].

**Top Opportunity:** cite Retro's download-count growth and subscription model[1][2][3] as the
next tier of external validation for Pillar 1 — a second, independently-dated confirmation of a
funded "anti-feed" competitor is a stronger citation for a Reddit devlog/postmortem post than a
single funding-announcement mention, consistent with how Yope's own entry was built up over two
runs before being cited as settled evidence.

## 3. Competitive Edge

1. **What are they doing that we aren't?** Retro — not a direct mechanic competitor, but the same
   "private beats public" macro-thesis bracket as Yope — has gone from a funding announcement
   (Aug 2026) to a growth figure (7M downloads by October) with a working subscription model[1][2][3].
   No named watchlist competitor (Yope, Zentara, ZenFriend, Coverstar, Highrise/ZEPETO) shipped
   anything materially new this week — Zentara and ZenFriend returned zero news results again,
   the same null pattern since 2026-09-18.
2. **Is this gap worth closing for us?** Not a feature gap — Retro's mechanic (friend-capped photo
   journal with physical postcard mailing) is a different mechanic than RabbitLore's peer
   lore-matching, same "track the thesis, not the feature" call already made for Yope
   (`research/2026-10-01.md`). What's new is evidentiary strength: this run has a second,
   independently-dated confirmation of Retro's growth, not just the initial raise.
3. **What's the concrete move?** (a) Add Retro to `competitors/watchlist.md` as a new, tracked
   (not chased) entry — same treatment as Yope: real external validation of the macro-thesis, no
   feature to copy. (b) Add Retro's growth/subscription figures to `strategy.md` §4 Pillar 1 as a
   second independently-funded "anti-feed" proof point alongside Yope's. (c) Note MIDiA's
   entertainment-vs-connection bifurcation finding[4] as a balancing caution in Pillar 1 — don't
   let future copy overclaim "private is simply winning," the real picture is a split market and
   RabbitLore sits in one half of it. (d) ZYMIX is explicitly NOT added as a watchlist competitor
   — opposite mechanic (consolidation/superapp, not atomization/privacy), different audience (UK
   university students, payments-led), and its one dated news angle (the blacklisted-server
   security story[6]) is a trust liability for that product specifically, not a feature gap for
   RabbitLore to close. (e) Recommend to this week's campaign-workflow run: Retro's download-
   growth and subscription-model figures are a stronger, more concrete citation than a bare
   funding-round mention for the next Reddit devlog angle on the anti-feed thesis.
