# RabbitLore Research Findings — 2026-10-01

## Executive Summary & Opportunity

**New named competitor this week: Yope** — London-based private "micro-community" social app,
closed a $12.3M Series A (led by Northzone, announced 2026-07-22), now at ~15M users and half a
billion shared photos since its 2024 launch, on zero marketing spend [1][2]. Yope's own framing —
"no algorithms, no ads, no public content," accounts private by default, built because "30% of
young people" were already using private spam/photo-dump accounts to share candidly with small
real-life-friend groups while avoiding public posting [1] — is the closest competitor positioning
RabbitLore has seen yet to its own "not social media... not followers... not virality" stance
(`docs/positioning.md`). This is a real, well-funded, fast-growing player validating the exact
macro thesis RabbitLore's strategy is built on (private > public for Gen Z), not a feature
RabbitLore is missing — see Competitive Edge below for why this changes urgency, not direction.

Also new: real 2026 news corroboration of the "Gen Z pulling back from public social media" thesis
— an ABC News piece (2026-09-21) on Gen Z actively fighting phone/social-media dependency [4], and
a Harris Poll/Jonathan Haidt-team survey finding 40% of Gen Z (ages 18-27) wish social media had
never been invented [3], cited by a competing "in-person-first" startup (Ville) using the same
data to justify its own anti-feed positioning [3]. Google Trends: "faceless creator app" interest,
flagged rising last week (0→14→12), dropped back to 0 this week (Sep 27–Oct 3) — a 2-week spike
that did not sustain into a 3rd week; read as a decayed single-event signal, not a hardening trend.
A second plausible proxy — "close friends app" — has been non-zero all year (21–48 range, no clear
trend direction week to week) — a steadier but flatter signal than the one-off spike.

**Top Opportunity:** The Yope funding/traction story is the single most important finding this
week — not because it changes what RabbitLore should build (nothing here contradicts the 4
existing pillars), but because it is hard evidence that a well-capitalized competitor is already
proving the "private, algorithm-free, non-public" model works at scale with Gen Z. See Competitive
Edge for the concrete, scoped move.

## 3. Competitive Edge

1. **What are they doing that we aren't?** Yope has *proof at scale* that RabbitLore only has as a
   thesis: 15M users, half a billion photos shared, zero marketing spend, built entirely on the
   premise that young people want private, algorithm-free, non-public sharing — the same premise
   `docs/positioning.md` states ("Not social media. Not algorithms. Not followers. Not
   virality.") [1][2]. Yope also ships a concrete AI feature RabbitLore doesn't have: AI-generated
   "memory recaps" and AI-assisted mini-games played with real friends inside the private space
   [1][2] — not avatar customization, but a different kind of in-app content RabbitLore's pillars
   don't currently cover.
2. **Is this gap worth closing for us?** The *positioning* gap is not a gap at all — it's
   independent validation RabbitLore should treat as reassurance, not a threat to copy. The
   *feature* gap (AI memory recaps / mini-games) is NOT worth chasing right now: Yope is a
   $12.3M-funded, 15M-user photo/group-chat product solving a different core loop (friend-group
   photo sharing) than RabbitLore's (`getBlend` peer lore-matching + night-gated story-sharing,
   per `rabbithole` commit `7c4e05a`) — building a copycat AI-recap feature would blur RabbitLore's
   actual differentiator (peer-to-peer lore recognition, not friend-group photo albums) for a
   feature that serves someone else's core loop, not ours. Positioning.md's audience (overthinkers,
   writers, people who "carry entire worlds in their heads") is also narrower and more specific
   than Yope's general "friends and family micro-community" target — don't widen to compete head-on.
3. **What's the concrete move?** Add Yope (and the 3 adjacent apps: Locket Widget, noplace, Ville)
   to the watchlist as a new tracked category — "the private-social wave" — distinct from the
   existing 3 named direct competitors (Zentara, ZenFriend, Highrise/ZEPETO), because they compete
   on macro-thesis (private > public) rather than on RabbitLore's specific mechanic (peer lore-
   matching + avatar-as-record). No new content pillar, no campaign change this week — this is a
   watchlist addition + a flag for the next strategy review to confirm Pillar 1's "anti-virality"
   framing still differentiates against a now-much-larger pool of private-social entrants, not
   just the 3 original named competitors. Concrete action for Vishal: worth a 5-minute look at
   Yope's actual app (it's free to try) before the next campaign cycle, specifically to see
   whether its "memory recap" AI feature changes how RabbitLore should talk about its own
   constellation star-map (Pillar 2) as a comparable-but-different "your private history,
   visualized" feature category — a product question, not a copy question, flagging rather than
   deciding here.
