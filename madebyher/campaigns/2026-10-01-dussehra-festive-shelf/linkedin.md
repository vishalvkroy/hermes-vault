## Post copy

19 days. That's the gap between Dussehra, October 20, and Diwali, November 8, this year - a real
calendar shift, since an extra lunar month pushed Diwali later than usual.

iTokri noticed the same shift. They opened their Festive 2026 collection on September 28, organized
by craft technique: Bandhani here, Banarasi there, every shelf named by its weaving method. It's a
smart content system for a pan-India catalog with hundreds of techniques to show off.

We sell from one state. Everything on our shelf right now is Madhubani, painted by hand by a woman
in Bihar, not sourced as inventory from a cluster three states away. So instead of organizing by
technique, we organized by festival: a puja table mat, two Madhubani coaster sets, one desk
organizer, four real products, four real artisans, live on the site today.

iTokri's approach works because their catalog is wide. Ours works because it isn't. A narrower,
single-region shelf is the actual product of a Bihar-only marketplace, not a smaller version of a
pan-India one.

If you run a regional or craft-specific store, how do you decide what NOT to carry as the catalog grows?

Building MadeByHer, a women-led artisanal marketplace from Bihar.
