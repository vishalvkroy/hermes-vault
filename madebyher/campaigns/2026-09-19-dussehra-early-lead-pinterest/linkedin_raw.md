19 days. That's the gap between Dussehra (Oct 20) and Diwali (Nov 8) this year.

Dussehra is usually treated as a minor checkpoint before the real festive season starts. For a
women-led artisan marketplace sourcing from Bihar, it's actually the production deadline. Once
Dussehra passes, there isn't enough runway left to finish a Diwali order that hasn't started
cutting, painting, or stitching yet - Madhubani pieces, crochet decor, and gift sets all take
real days on a real artisan's table, not a factory line that scales on demand.

We're building this year's Diwali gifting collection around that 19-day number now, a full month
out, instead of waiting for the usual pre-Diwali rush. It's early by our own past cadence - most
years we start real production planning inside three weeks of Diwali, not five.

If you work with artisan or handmade supply chains in India, curious how you're planning around
a compressed calendar like this one.
