## Post copy

19 days. That's the gap between Dussehra (Oct 20) and Diwali (Nov 8) this year.

Dussehra usually reads as a minor checkpoint before the real festive season starts. For a
women-led artisan marketplace sourcing from Bihar, it's the production deadline. Once Dussehra
passes, there isn't enough runway left to finish a Diwali order that hasn't started cutting,
painting, or stitching yet. Madhubani pieces, crochet decor, and gift sets take real days on a
real artisan's table. There's no factory line to scale on demand when the order comes in late.

We're building this year's Diwali gifting collection around that 19-day number now, a full month
out. Most years we start real production planning inside three weeks of Diwali, not five. This
year we moved it up on purpose, because 19 days isn't enough room for last-minute sourcing.

If you work with artisan or handmade supply chains in India, how are you planning around a
compressed calendar like this one?

P.S. Dussehra to Diwali is 19 days this year. Diwali to Chhath (Nov 13-16) is another 5. Three
festivals inside a month, one production window.
