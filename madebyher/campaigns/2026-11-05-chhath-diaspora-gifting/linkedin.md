100 to 200 bamboo trays, shipped from Bhagalpur to the US, for a festival most of the world has never heard of.

Those are real Chhath Puja "soops," hand-painted in the Manjusha style, ordered this season by Bihari families abroad who can't get home for the four days of fasting and river worship. Patna Press reported the number this week. Small count. Not nothing - someone in Texas or New Jersey paid to ship a ritual tray across an ocean rather than skip the puja.

A Rukam Capital and YouGov survey of over 4,000 young Indians backs this up: 74% buy festival gifts for parents and family, and the trend a Rukam Capital partner named specifically wasn't "mithai" in general - it was "regional specialities and fusion mithai."

MadeByHer is built on that specific-ness. Every seller is a named woman running her own kitchen or craft table in Bihar, not a factory label. Our thekua already ships pan-India, and the orders that stand out aren't the ones going three streets over in Patna - they're the ones going to someone who moved away and still wants Chhathi Maiya's prasad to taste like home.

Festivals don't stay local anymore. People move. The food and the craft are what travel back to them.
