195. That's how many times last month someone searched "pedakiya sweet bihar" on Google and
landed on our site.

Not "gift box." Not "artisan sweets." A dialect word for a snack most people outside Bihar have
never heard of.

We checked the data behind it. Thekua turns out to be the only regional Indian sweet that shows
real purchase activity in every single state and union territory. Not because it went viral.
Because Bihari and Purvanchali families who moved to Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, and abroad still
want it, and most of them can't get it made the way their mother made it.

One founder duo built that insight into a ₹75 crore business, from a home kitchen, by shipping a
festival sweet pan-India, year-round, instead of only during Chhath week.

We're not chasing that number. We're doing something narrower: putting one woman artisan's
actual thekua, made with real desi ghee and jaggery in her own kitchen in Bihar, in front of
people who are searching for exactly this and don't know it exists yet.

The gap between "everyone knows the demand exists" and "someone actually built the supply chain
for it" is where most real business ideas quietly die. This one didn't have to be huge to matter
to us. It just had to be real.

What's the smallest, most specific search term in your own analytics that's been trying to tell
you something for months?

P.S. If you're a Bihari or Purvanchali artisan making something like this and you're not
selling it outside your local market yet, that's the actual gap we built MadeByHer to close.
