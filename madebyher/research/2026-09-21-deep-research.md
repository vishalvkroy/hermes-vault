## Executive Summary
Festive artisanal demand is shifting toward conscious gifting, heritage storytelling, and regional authenticity for upcoming Diwali (Nov 1) and Chhath festivals [1]. Major news and competitor signals indicate high traction for Bihari artisanal goods (e.g., Sattuz enterprise scaling globally, Lac bangles facing Middle East supply bottlenecks) [2][3].

## 2. Market & Trend Signals
- **Festive Handmade Revival:** Business Standard reports a massive return to traditional Indian handicrafts for Diwali gifting, with families moving away from mass-produced imports toward heritage stoles, terracotta, and brass artifacts [1].
- **Bihari Artisan Success Stories:** 'Sattuz' (founded by Sachin Kumar & Richa Kumari from Patna) crossed ₹5 crore in enterprise value, gaining traction post-BRICS and exporting globally while retaining the "Made in Bihar" tag [2].
- **Supply Chain Friction:** Muzaffarpur’s Islampur Lahthi (lac bangle) market faces a 25–30% price hike and supply bottleneck due to West Asian trade route disruptions affecting decorative stones and chemicals [3].
- **Competitor Moves (Hastvikas):** Launched augmented reality (AR) product visualization ("See how it looks in your home") and transparency initiatives ("100% Authentic with Artisan name") [5].

## 3. Competitive Edge & Actionable Strategy
1. **What are they doing that we aren't?** Competitor Hastvikas deployed AR visualization and prominent artisan attribution ("Authentic with Artisan name") [5]. 
2. **Is that gap worth closing?** Yes. Transparent artisan attribution directly reinforces MadeByHer's core mission of women-led rural empowerment in Bihar.
3. **Concrete Move:** Update product listings for high-momentum items (`pedakiya`, `thekua`, crochet bags) to include explicit artisan profile cards and transparent regional provenance. Lean into the "Made in Bihar" narrative to capture conscious gifting search intent.
