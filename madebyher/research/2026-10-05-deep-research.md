# MadeByHer Research Findings — 2026-10-05

## High-impact opportunity: turn search demand into giftable product pages and Pinterest pins

MadeByHer has live search demand for giftable, visual products: the 7-day GSC report shows `crochet rose potli bag` at 13 impressions, `crochet rose potli bag price` at 8, `daisy flower keychain` at 11, and `daisy keychain` at 11, while the product page `/product/handmade-crochet-rose-drawstring-mini-potli-bag` has 96 impressions and only 1 click in the same 7-day window.[12] This is more actionable than generic “handmade gifts” content because it points to real MadeByHer inventory and Pinterest-friendly product visuals.[12]

**Concrete move this week:** create a product SEO + Pinterest bundle for crochet potli bags and daisy keychains: update titles/meta/alt text, create 3–5 fresh 2:3 Pinterest pins per product, and make one Instagram carousel around “handmade crochet gifts under ₹1,500 from Bihar”. The brand already promises handmade gifts, custom orders, Cash on Delivery, and pan-India shipping, so this does not invent a new claim.[1]

## 3. Niche trends & market signals

India’s gifting market page estimates 2026 gifting at ₹61,000–65,000 crore, says Diwali drives 30–35% of annual spend, and lists sweets/dry fruits/food hampers as 22–25% of gifting value.[5] This supports MadeByHer’s food-led festival pages, but GSC says current on-site search demand is more specific than broad “Diwali gifts”: anarsa, pedakiya, thekua-price, and product pages are where search impressions already show up.[12]

The same gifting source says personalised/custom gifts are growing fastest among listed categories at +34% for 2024–26.[5] MadeByHer already invites wedding favours, return gifts, custom colours, and WhatsApp special requests, so custom crochet/potli/keychain positioning is supported by both external trend and internal product positioning.[1][5]

Reddit India Diwali discussions still skew practical and meaningful: one thread asks for a meaningful Diwali gift for an Indian partner and suggestions include pottery, jewellery/clothes, photo keepsakes, and sweets.[9] Another r/india Diwali discussion explicitly urges people to buy from small vendors and handicraft workers rather than large chains.[14] These support MadeByHer’s “buy from women-led small businesses” Diwali message.

## 4. News and articles

PIB says eSARAS is an official DAY-NRLM marketplace for women SHG products, with ONDC availability across 11+ buyer apps and 800+ handcrafted products listed.[6] This confirms institutional marketplaces are moving toward ONDC-enabled distribution for rural women makers, so MadeByHer should keep ONDC as a medium-term distribution test rather than treat its own website as the only channel.[6]

Indian Startup Times reports Artisans’ Wizard has onboarded over 12,000 sellers and is using ONDC to solve visibility, accessibility for non-GST sellers, and low-commission economics for small manufacturers.[7] This is directly relevant to MadeByHer’s `/sell` page because GSC already shows seller-acquisition intent converting better than `/shop`.[12]

## 5. Competitor moves

Weavehand is newly worth tracking. Its site positions itself as “India’s Artisan Empowerment Ecosystem,” claims 1,000+ verified artisans, 10K+ handmade products, and 38+ districts, and has explicit “Corporate Gifts” and “Festivals” categories.[2] Its about page says it offers hand-signed certificates of authenticity, direct artisan messaging for custom requests, GI-tag certification on eligible pieces, eco-friendly packaging, multi-currency checkout, international shipping, and planned physical Cultural Hubs.[3]

JEEViKA/Shilpgram remains a Bihar women-artisan benchmark: it says it is headquartered in Darbhanga and collectively owned/run by 500+ skilled rural women artisans under BRLPS/JEEViKA.[4] This reinforces MadeByHer’s “Bihar women-led” positioning, but also shows government-backed players own institutional credibility.[4]

## Competitive Edge

### 1. Highest-priority gap: productized gift bundles, not just marketplace browsing

Weavehand already exposes “Corporate Gifts” and “Festivals” as shopping categories and sells a customizable Tikuli Art gift box.[2] India gifting data says sweets/food hampers are 22–25% of gifting value and personalised/custom gifts show +34% growth for 2024–26.[5] MadeByHer has the ingredients — crochet, thekua/nimki, custom colours, WhatsApp special requests — but the site’s `/shop` search CTR is only 0.58% over 7 days while individual product/category intent is visible.[1][12]

**Worth closing?** Yes. This gap maps directly to real MadeByHer inventory and this week’s GSC product queries, not a speculative channel.[12]

**Concrete move:** create “Handmade crochet gifts under ₹1,500” and “Bihar festive food gifts” collection/pin sets, not broad `/shop` pushes. Use product names in titles: “crochet rose potli bag”, “daisy keychain”, “thekua gift pack”, “anarsa online”.[12]

### 2. Strategic gap: ONDC/non-GST seller distribution

eSARAS and Artisans’ Wizard both frame ONDC as a way to get rural or small sellers onto national buyer apps with less dependence on private marketplaces.[6][7] MadeByHer’s `/sell` page is already its second-best organic page this week, with 14.3% CTR over 7 days.[12]

**Worth closing?** Medium-term yes, but not this week’s campaign priority. ONDC is operationally heavier than SEO/Pinterest fixes.

**Concrete move:** keep ONDC as a proposed experiment in strategy.md, but this week run lighter seller-acquisition content: “sell handmade items online free in India” maps to a real 7-day GSC query with 1 click / 1 impression at position 1.[12]
