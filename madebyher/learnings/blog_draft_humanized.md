# How to Order Authentic Thekua Online: A Complete Buying Guide

Thekua has traveled far from its roots as a Chhath Puja offering in Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh. Today it shows up in dessert boxes across Bangalore, gets ordered for office gifting in Mumbai, and ships to diaspora kitchens in the US and UK. GoKwik's 2024 data puts it as the top regional sweet ordered in every Indian state, with the biggest buyers outside its home region.

If you want the real thing — crisp, not stale, made with jaggery and ghee, not shortcuts — here is what to check before you place a thekua online order.

## What makes thekua authentic

Traditional thekua has three non-negotiables: whole wheat flour, jaggery (not refined sugar), and ghee. The dough is kneaded stiff, pressed into wooden molds called *saancha* that imprint geometric or floral patterns, then deep-fried in ghee or mustard oil until the edges lace brown. No baking powder. No preservatives. The texture should shatter cleanly, not bend.

Variations exist. Some families add coconut, fennel seeds, or cardamom. Chhath thekua tends to be larger and plainer, meant for the *arghya* offering. Festive versions for gifting are smaller, often decorated with silver leaf (*varak*). Both are valid. What matters is the ingredient list reads like a pantry, not a lab.

## Why buying online is different from buying local

In Bihar, you buy from a *halwai* whose family has made thekua for generations. You see the batch, smell the ghee, judge the color. Online, you rely on signals: photos, descriptions, reviews, and the seller's transparency about process.

Three things degrade in transit: crispness from humidity, aroma from time, and shape from packing. A good online seller accounts for all three.

## How to spot quality in a listing

Start with the ingredient list. Look for: whole wheat flour, jaggery, ghee, water. Optional: coconut, fennel, cardamom, silver leaf. Red flags: refined flour (maida), sugar, vegetable oil, preservatives (E-numbers), artificial flavor.

Process photos matter. Legitimate makers show the *saancha* molds, the frying kadhai, women shaping dough. Stock photos of generic sweets suggest a reseller, not a maker.

Shelf life claims tell a story. Authentic thekua lasts 3 to 4 weeks in airtight tins at room temperature. Anyone claiming 2 to 3 months likely uses preservatives or industrial packaging that traps moisture.

Packaging details. Metal tins beat plastic boxes beat polybags. Tins block light and air. Some sellers vacuum-seal inside the tin, ideal for long-distance shipping.

Origin specificity. "Made in Bihar" is vague. "Made by women artisans in Muzaffarpur" or "sourced from Bhusura village, Sujani cluster" is traceable. MadeByHer lists the artisan collective and district for every food product.

Weight and count clarity. Listings should state net weight and approximate piece count. A 400g tin typically holds 20 to 24 pieces. Vague "family pack" without grams is a skip.

## Shipping and freshness

Thekua travels well — it was made for storage. But heat and humidity are enemies.

- Summer months (April to June): Choose sellers who ship in insulated boxes with cold packs, or opt for express delivery (24 to 48 hours).
- Monsoon: Ensure the tin is vacuum-sealed or has a tight rubber gasket. Moisture ruins crispness in hours.
- International: Check customs rules for ghee-based sweets. Some countries restrict dairy. Sellers experienced with diaspora orders usually declare correctly and use food-grade tins that clear inspection.

Ask the seller their dispatch day. Many artisanal makers bake to order — they fry on Monday, pack Tuesday, ship Wednesday. A 3 to 4 day lead time often means fresher product than "instant dispatch" from a warehouse.

## Common questions

**Is thekua gluten-free?** No. Whole wheat flour is essential to the structure. No authentic gluten-free version exists.

**Can I freeze it?** Yes. Freeze in the sealed tin up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature 2 hours before opening — do not microwave.

**Why does my thekua taste different from last time?** Jaggery varies by season and source. Summer gur is lighter; winter gur is darker, more mineral. Artisanal batches reflect this. Industrial batches standardize with additives.

**What if it arrives soft?** Humidity during transit. Spread pieces on a plate, leave uncovered in an air-conditioned room for 4 to 6 hours. They often re-crisp. If not, the batch was under-fried — contact the seller.
